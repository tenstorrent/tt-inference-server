SCENE. Amsterdam, the Prinsengracht canal, late dusk in early autumn. The sky holds a gradient from deep ultramarine overhead to a bruised amber at the rooftops. Canal houses lean shoulder to shoulder, their gabled silhouettes doubled in the still water. Windows glow one by one. A thin mist sits a hand's width above the canal surface and drifts with the current. The street lamps are the old cast-iron kind; their sodium light pools on wet cobblestones that were rained on an hour ago and have not yet dried.

CHARACTERS. The first is an old man, late sixties, white collar-length hair, a short grey beard, deep-set tired eyes. He wears a blue-and-grey plaid flannel shirt under a worn olive field jacket with a torn left pocket, dark work trousers, and scuffed leather boots. He walks with a slight favor to his right knee, hands half in his pockets, and he watches the water more than the path. The second is a robot, two heads taller than the man, built from salvaged plate steel with mismatched panels, faded warning decals, and traces of pink graffiti on its left shoulder. Its head is a blunt wedge with two dim white eyes and articulated shutter-jaws. Cables run like tendons along its neck. It moves with heavy, deliberate steps, hydraulics breathing at each stride, and it keeps exactly two paces behind the man at all times.

SHOT LIST, SECOND BY SECOND. 0.0 to 3.0 seconds: wide establishing shot from across the canal, the pair entering frame left, walking right, their reflections stretched and broken by ripples; a tram bell rings far away. 3.0 to 6.0 seconds: cut to a low tracking shot ahead of them on the same bank, dolly moving backward at walking pace, the man's boots and the robot's steel feet hitting the cobbles in alternating rhythm, a puddle splash catching lamplight. 6.0 to 9.0 seconds: medium two-shot from the side, the man stops at a rusted railing, rests both hands on it, and looks down at the water; the robot halts a beat later, servos settling with a descending whine; a houseboat window flickers with television light behind them. 9.0 to 12.0 seconds: slow push-in from medium to close-up on the man's face, mist drifting between camera and subject, his breath faintly visible, his eyes following something unseen beneath the surface; the robot's dim eyes reflect in the water beside his reflection. 12.0 to 15.0 seconds: the man turns his head toward the robot, the faintest beginning of a smile, and the robot tilts its wedge head three degrees in answer; hold the two-shot as a barge horn sounds long and low from the harbor, and cut to black on the horn's decay.

CAMERA AND LENS. Shoot as if on anamorphic glass, 40 millimeter equivalent, wide open around T2.2 so the background lamps bloom into soft horizontal ovals. Keep camera height at the man's chest for every ground shot. Movement is always motivated and smooth, gimbal-steady with a barely perceptible handheld sway, never a whip pan, never a zoom. Depth of field stays shallow on faces and deep on the establishing wide. Flares from the sodium lamps may kiss the frame edges but must never cross a face.

LIGHT AND GRADE. Three light families only: the warm sodium street lamps at 2200 kelvin, the cool ambient dusk sky at 9000 kelvin, and the intermittent domestic warmth from canal-house windows. Faces are lit by lamp light from the left, rimmed by sky light from the right. The grade is filmic with lifted blacks, gentle halation on highlights, fine organic grain, muted greens, and skin tones protected from the orange cast. No crushed shadows, no clipped windows, no digital sharpness.

SOUND DESIGN, LAYER BY LAYER. Base layer: low city room-tone, distant traffic wash, and the specific hollow lap of canal water against stone every few seconds. Mid layer: the alternating footsteps, leather boot soft and steel foot heavy, kept slightly left of center; the robot's hydraulic exhale on each stride; the creak of the railing when the man leans on it. Event layer: one far tram bell at the second mark, a bicycle freewheel passing behind camera at the seventh second, a houseboat television murmuring indistinctly, and the single long barge horn beginning at the thirteenth second and decaying into the cut. Music: a sparse string quartet in D minor, cello entering first at the fourth second, violins joining at the ninth, rising to a suspended unresolved chord that hands the ending to the barge horn. Dialogue: none. The mix keeps music ten decibels under the horn at the climax.

TEXTURE AND CONTINUITY NOTES. The mist thickens very slightly over the fifteen seconds. Ripples from a passing duck reach the pair's reflections at the eighth second. The robot's left shoulder graffiti must remain visible in every side angle. The man's jacket stays open. Nothing in the frame is modern glass-and-steel; every facade is brick, every window frame painted wood. No on-screen text, no logos, no other pedestrians closer than the far background, where two cyclists cross the humpback bridge at the eleventh second, unhurried. The overall mood is quiet companionship at the end of a long day, tenderness without sentimentality, machine and man sharing the same tiredness and the same view of the water.
